World Weather Attribution 23 JUL
Climate Change Made Europe's 2026 Soil Droughts 5× More Likely in the West and 11× More Likely in the East — WWA Rapid Attribution

A World Weather Attribution rapid attribution study published July 23 found that climate change has made the severe soil moisture droughts intensifying beneath the 2026 European heatwave at least five times more likely in western Europe and approximately eleven times more likely in eastern Europe. The study combines observational soil moisture records with large multi-model climate ensembles, separately attributing changes in drought frequency and intensity across western Europe (France, Spain, Portugal) and eastern Europe. In the west, the current drought levels would have been classified as exceptional in historical frequency under pre-industrial climate conditions; in the east, the same soil moisture depletion would have been statistically extraordinary. The WWA team projects that soil droughts of this severity will become a near-annual occurrence across both regions under a +2°C global warming scenario — conditions on track to be reached within roughly 20 years under current emissions trajectories. The finding directly conditions both the France and Spain wildfire emergencies, where record-low summer soil moisture has produced an extraordinarily dense fuel load even in regions with historically lower fire risk.

WWA (23 июля): изменение климата сделало текущие почвенные засухи не менее чем в 5 раз более вероятными в Западной Европе (Франция, Испания, Португалия) и в ~11 раз — в Восточной. На основе данных о влажности почвы и мультимодельных ансамблей. На западе засуха этого уровня была бы «исключительной» при доиндустриальном климате; на востоке — статистически невозможной. При потеплении +2°C события такой тяжести станут ежегодными. Дефицит влажности почвы напрямую обусловил рекордную пожарную нагрузку во Франции и Испании.

World Weather Attribution published a rapid attribution study on July 16 analyzing the extreme rainfall events driving the ongoing floods in Ivory Coast, Ghana, and Togo. The team found that climate change has made heavy rainfall events of the observed intensity approximately 1.4 times more intense and 1.6 times more frequent in the West Africa region compared to pre-industrial conditions, consistent with the Clausius–Clapeyron thermodynamic expectation of approximately 7% additional atmospheric moisture per degree of warming. Sea surface temperatures in the Gulf of Guinea were significantly above average during the event, amplifying moisture convergence. The study notes that improved long-term rainfall observations across the Sahel and Gulf of Guinea are needed to reduce uncertainty bounds, which remain larger than in well-observed European or North American attribution contexts, while highlighting that the attribution signal — climate change increasing the likelihood of such events — is robust across the available model ensemble.

Изменение климата сделало подобные ливни в 1,4 раза интенсивнее и в 1,6 раза более частыми по сравнению с доиндустриальным климатом — соответствует уравнению Клаузиуса–Клапейрона (~7% влаги/°C). ТПО Гвинейского залива выше нормы усиливала конвергенцию влаги. GraphCast Evaluated for Indian Summer Monsoon: Competitive Large-Scale Skill but Systematic Wet Bias Over Subcontinent Found

arXiv:2607.11905 · physics.ao-ph · submitted July 2026

Researchers conduct a systematic evaluation of Google DeepMind's GraphCast AI weather model for Indian Summer Monsoon (ISM) prediction in arXiv:2607.11905, comparing against ECMWF IFS and NCEP GFS across multiple monsoon seasons. The study finds GraphCast demonstrates a systematic wet bias over the Indian subcontinent during the core monsoon months (June–September), particularly in the central Indian monsoon region where variability is dominated by synoptic-scale systems and the Boreal Summer Intraseasonal Oscillation. Despite this bias, GraphCast showed competitive skill for large-scale ISM onset timing and 850-hPa monsoon wind forecasts at medium-range lead times. The authors recommend targeted bias correction post-processing and fine-tuning on monsoon-season training data to close the wet-bias gap, noting that the ISM represents a high-priority benchmark for AI models given its societal importance and the complexity of its multi-scale interactions from planetary to mesoscale.

arXiv:2607.11905: систематическая оценка GraphCast (Google DeepMind) для прогнозирования Индийского летнего муссона (ISM). Обнаружен систематический влажный уклон (wet bias) над Индией в муссонные месяцы (июнь–сентябрь), особенно выраженный в центральном муссонном регионе (влияние синоптических систем и БSISO). При этом GraphCast конкурентоспособен по прогнозированию сроков начала ISM и ветра 850 гПа. Авторы рекомендуют коррекцию смещения и дообучение на муссонных данных; ISM — ключевой бенчмарк для ИИ-моделей.
