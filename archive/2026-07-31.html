World Weather Attribution 23 JUL
Climate Change Made Europe's 2026 Soil Droughts 5× More Likely in the West and 11× More Likely in the East — WWA Rapid Attribution

A World Weather Attribution rapid attribution study published July 23 found that climate change has made the severe soil moisture droughts intensifying beneath the 2026 European heatwave at least five times more likely in western Europe and approximately eleven times more likely in eastern Europe. The study combines observational soil moisture records with large multi-model climate ensembles, separately attributing changes in drought frequency and intensity across western Europe (France, Spain, Portugal) and eastern Europe. In the west, the current drought levels would have been classified as exceptional in historical frequency under pre-industrial climate conditions; in the east, the same soil moisture depletion would have been statistically extraordinary. The WWA team projects that soil droughts of this severity will become a near-annual occurrence across both regions under a +2°C global warming scenario — conditions on track to be reached within roughly 20 years under current emissions trajectories. The finding directly conditions both the France and Spain wildfire emergencies, where record-low summer soil moisture has produced an extraordinarily dense fuel load even in regions with historically lower fire risk.

WWA (23 июля): изменение климата сделало текущие почвенные засухи не менее чем в 5 раз более вероятными в Западной Европе (Франция, Испания, Португалия) и в ~11 раз — в Восточной. На основе данных о влажности почвы и мультимодельных ансамблей. На западе засуха этого уровня была бы «исключительной» при доиндустриальном климате; на востоке — статистически невозможной. При потеплении +2°C события такой тяжести станут ежегодными. Дефицит влажности почвы напрямую обусловил рекордную пожарную нагрузку во Франции и Испании.
