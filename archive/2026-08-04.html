ScienceDaily / GRL 01 AUG
Scientists Detect Sharp Acceleration in Global Warming Rate — 0.35°C/Decade Since 2015 vs. 0.2°C/Decade Prior; Paris 1.5°C Threshold ~2030

A study published in Geophysical Research Letters by Grant Foster and Stefan Rahmstorf (Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research) finds that the global mean surface temperature warming rate has sharply accelerated since approximately 2015, rising to 0.35°C per decade — nearly double the 0.2°C per decade pace observed from 1970 to 2015. The authors demonstrate the acceleration with greater than 98% statistical confidence, ruling out internal variability as the primary driver and attributing the shift to continued greenhouse gas forcing combined with the reduction of cooling sulfate aerosol emissions from shipping following 2020 IMO regulations. At the current accelerated pace, the 1.5°C warming threshold specified in the Paris Agreement is projected to be crossed around 2030, roughly five years earlier than most prior central estimates.

Исследование GRL (Foster & Rahmstorf, PIK): скорость глобального потепления с ~2015 г. составляет 0,35°C/дек. против 0,2°C/дек. в 1970–2015 гг. Достоверность превышения >98%; основной фактор — парниковые газы плюс сокращение охлаждающих сульфатных аэрозолей после правил ИМО-2020. При нынешнем темпе порог 1,5°C будет пройден около 2030 г. — на ~5 лет раньше прежних медианных оценок.

GRL / PIK 01 AUG
GRL: Global Warming Rate Has Nearly Doubled Since 2015 — Foster & Rahmstorf Detect 0.35°C/Decade vs. 0.2°C/Decade Prior, >98% Confidence (GRL 53:5, 10.1029/2025GL118804)

Grant Foster and Stefan Rahmstorf (Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research) publish in Geophysical Research Letters (53:5, DOI 10.1029/2025GL118804) a statistical analysis of the global mean surface temperature record through mid-2026, finding a statistically significant acceleration of the warming trend that began around 2015. The pre-2015 warming rate from 1970–2015 was approximately 0.2°C per decade; the post-2015 rate is approximately 0.35°C per decade — a near-doubling. The authors demonstrate the acceleration with greater than 98% statistical confidence using multiple independent methods, ruling out El Niño alone as the explanation and attributing the step-change in forcing to the combined effect of continued CO₂ accumulation and the 2020 IMO sulfur regulations that reduced the cooling masking effect of shipping aerosols. At the accelerated pace, the 1.5°C Paris threshold is projected around 2030.
