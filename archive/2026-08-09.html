AP / Las Vegas Sun 05 AUG
This Summer Isn't Breaking Heat Records Globally — Yet. Scientists Warn the Real Records Arrive When El Niño Peaks This Fall

An AP analysis explains why 2026 is currently tracking as the third-warmest year on record rather than the hottest: the El Niño signal propagates to global-mean atmospheric temperature with a roughly six-to-eight-week lag behind its ocean forcing, meaning the full impact of the event building in the tropical Pacific won't register in surface temperature anomalies until Q4. Scientists project that once the lag resolves, the compounding effect of long-term warming and the intensifying El Niño will produce a surge in global mean temperature. Ocean temperatures are already showing the signal: global SSTs have been at or above 2024 record levels nearly every day since June, and 82% of the ocean surface is currently experiencing marine heatwave conditions per Copernicus Marine.

AP объясняет, почему 2026 г. пока на третьем месте, а не на первом: атмосферный отклик на Эль-Ниньо запаздывает на 6–8 недель. Когда задержка разрешится в Q4, эффект накопится. Уже сейчас SST на уровне рекордов 2024 года или выше — почти каждый день с июня; 82% поверхности океана — в режиме морских волн тепла по данным Copernicus Marine.
