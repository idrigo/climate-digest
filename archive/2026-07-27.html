The plan adds dispatch requirements alongside the capacity target: by 2030, wind and solar backed by storage must be able to supply 8% of installed capacity during peak demand and cover 20% of electricity use during demand spikes; China also targets more than 300 GW of renewable peaking capacity by 2030. The plan arrives as China also began imposing consumption taxes on solar cells and lithium batteries to force manufacturing consolidation in severely overcrowded sectors.

China Imposes First-Ever Consumption Tax on Solar Cells and Lithium Batteries to Force Industry Consolidation

China ended a decade-long consumption tax exemption for solar cells and lithium-ion batteries on July 17, imposing a 2% tax on lithium batteries from September 2026 (rising to 4% in September 2027) and a 2% tax on solar cells from April 2027 (rising to 4% in April 2028). ECMWF's AIFS-DOP Produces 42-Year Global Reanalysis From Observations Alone — Without Physics Models — in a Single Working Day

ECMWF · arXiv:2607.07879 · physics.ao-ph · ECMWF news release July 22, 2026

ECMWF researchers trained AIFS-DOP — a variant of their Artificial Intelligence Forecasting System — end-to-end exclusively on sparse Earth system observations, with no reanalysis used as inputs or targets. As a proof of concept the team produced a prototype global reanalysis spanning 1981–2022 at approximately 112 km resolution with atmospheric state estimates every six hours; the entire 42-year dataset was generated in a single working day. Evaluations show root mean square vector error of upper-level winds close to that of ERA5 at matched resolution, and surface error between those of the 4th- and 5th-generation ECMWF reanalyses. The result opens a pathway to fast, cheap global reanalysis generation unconstrained by the computational cost of running physics-based data assimilation cycles — with potential to extend records into data-sparse eras where physics-based reanalyses struggle.

ECMWF обучил AIFS-DOP исключительно на разреженных наблюдениях (без реанализа) и за один рабочий день сгенерировал прототип 42-летнего глобального реанализа (1981–2022) с разрешением ~112 км и шагом 6 ч. RMSE ветра на верхних уровнях — близко к ERA5; ошибка у поверхности — между ERA4 и ERA5. Результат открывает путь к быстрому и дешёвому глобальному реанализу без затрат на физическое усвоение данных, в т.ч. для эпох с дефицитом наблюдений.

NSF NCAR's CAMulator AI Model Demonstrates Skill Above Dynamical Forecasts in the Subseasonal 2–8 Week Prediction Window

NSF NCAR · news release · phys.org · July 22, 2026

NSF NCAR researchers published a demonstration of CAMulator — the Community Atmosphere Model Emulator — applied to the subseasonal prediction problem (2 to 8 weeks ahead), the range long described as a "window of opportunity" where even the best dynamical models lose deterministic skill. In hindcast testing over 2020–2021, CAMulator showed statistically significant skill improvements over operational ensemble averages from ECMWF and NCEP GFS for 2-meter temperature, 500-hPa geopotential height, and precipitation anomalies at 2- and 3-week leads. The largest gains appear in regions where ENSO and MJO teleconnections dominate, as the model learns these large-scale drivers from training data rather than relying on explicit physical parameterizations. The framework is designed as a fast, computationally inexpensive post-processing emulator compatible with existing NWP pipelines.

NSF NCAR опубликовал CAMulator (эмулятор CAM) для субсезонного прогнозирования (2–8 недель — «окно возможностей»). В ретроспективном тестировании 2020–2021 гг. модель статистически значимо превзошла ансамбли ECMWF и GFS для T2m, z500 и осадков на горизонтах 2 и 3 недели. Наибольшие выигрыши — в регионах с сильными телесвязями ЭНСО и МДО. CAMulator — быстрый дешёвый постпроцессор для существующих NWP-пайплайнов.

Multivariate Scoring Rules Match or Beat CRPS for Probabilistic NWP Training, ECMWF Study Finds

ECMWF team · arXiv:2607.19161 · physics.ao-ph / cs.LG · also published in npj Artificial Intelligence · July 21, 2026

Researchers at ECMWF systematically compare versions of the AIFS-CRPS global probabilistic weather model trained on different univariate and multivariate scoring rules: the (nearly) fair CRPS, a fair global energy score, and a graph energy score based on local grid-node neighbourhood structure. In the extratropics all three objectives produce similar forecast skill; in the tropics the graph energy score outperforms CRPS while the global energy score shows some degradation — suggesting multivariate scoring rules are a viable and potentially superior alternative to CRPS-based training for global ML weather forecasting. The AIFS-CRPS architecture paper has concurrently been peer-reviewed in npj Artificial Intelligence, formalising the model's status alongside its operational deployment at ECMWF.

Greenland Meltwater Weakens AMOC Significantly But Keeps Overturning Well Clear of Tipping Point, GRL Study Finds

Mehling et al. · Geophysical Research Letters · DOI: 10.1029/2026GL122545 · Bergen / MPI-Hamburg · July 21, 2026

Dirk Mehling and colleagues at Bergen/MPI-Hamburg present freshwater hosing experiments using a high-resolution ocean model combined with CMIP6 projections of Greenland Ice Sheet mass loss under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, published in Geophysical Research Letters. Their results show that projected twenty-first century Greenland meltwater discharge weakens AMOC by approximately 1–3 Sv — a physically meaningful reduction but insufficient to trigger the self-amplifying collapse transition associated with the AMOC tipping point. The model ensemble consistently places AMOC above the critical stability threshold under all tested melt trajectories through 2100. The authors caution that the finding is robust to model spread within current projections, but the tipping point risk cannot be excluded at warming levels above 3°C where meltwater fluxes could exceed those sampled here.

Offshore Wind Construction Can Protect North Atlantic Right Whales at Minimal Cost, Nature Climate Change Study Finds

· Nature Climate Change · DOI: 10.1038/s41558-026-02696-9 · 2026

A modelling study in Nature Climate Change develops an optimization framework for protecting critically endangered North Atlantic right whales (NARW) during offshore wind construction off the US East Coast, using pile-driving noise simulation and whale density surface models across ten mitigation scenarios. The analysis finds that a combination of seasonal pile-driving restrictions — timed to avoid peak NARW habitat use periods — and noise-attenuating devices (bubble curtains or equivalent) can substantially reduce the number of whales exposed to harmful noise levels while imposing only marginal additional costs on project developers. The result directly addresses one of the principal regulatory barriers to US East Coast offshore wind deployment, where NARW protection requirements under the Marine Mammal Protection Act have delayed or blocked multiple projects.
