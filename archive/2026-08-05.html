The Conversation 05 AUG
Why the Spokane Wildfires Became So Catastrophic — Record Snowpack Deficit, Compound Drought, WUI Expansion, and the Heat Dome: A Forensic Analysis

The Conversation publishes a forensic climate-science analysis of the conditions that enabled the Spokane Area Fires to destroy 700+ structures and displace 65,000 people in less than 72 hours. The authors identify four converging factors: Pacific Northwest snowpack at its lowest levels in 40 years, eliminating the late-summer moisture buffer that historically moderated fire risk; a compound multi-year drought (2024 and 2025 both in the driest quartile for the region) that has desiccated surface fuels to critically low dead-fuel moisture levels; aggressive wildland-urban interface development that placed tens of thousands of structures directly in high-risk terrain with limited defensible space; and the sustained heat dome that drove temperatures to 100–118°F for over a week, reducing live-fuel moisture to record lows. The paper situates the Spokane event within the broader Pacific Northwest fire season (50+ large uncontained fires, worst season in 30 years) and argues that the scale of destruction was climatologically predictable given the compound preconditions.

The Conversation: климатологический анализ катастрофы в Спокане. Четыре сходящихся фактора: снежный покров — минимум за 40 лет (потеря позднелетнего увлажняющего буфера); двухлетняя составная засуха (2024 и 2025 гг. — в нижнем квартиле осадков); агрессивная застройка зон WUI без достаточных защитных полос; купол жары с температурами 38–48°C в течение недели. Авторы помещают Споукан в контекст всего сезона на Северо-Западе (50+ крупных пожаров, худший год за 30 лет) и указывают, что масштаб ущерба был климатологически предсказуем.

RenewablesВозобновляемая энергетика

The European battery storage fleet is about to face its most public real-world test: Ember reports that the EU's 29 GW battery fleet is primed for the August 12 solar eclipse, when the path of totality sweeps across Spain, France, and Italy between roughly 8:28 and 8:32 PM local time, temporarily reducing solar output by tens of gigawatts within a four-minute window. Unlike the 2015 and 2022 European eclipses — when battery capacity was negligible and grid operators relied on fast-ramping gas and hydro to absorb the solar dropout — the 2026 fleet is large enough to buffer the transition without thermal plant intervention, making August 12 a live demonstration of what storage-at-scale looks like on a continental grid. On the M&A front, TotalEnergies and Shell completed the largest European onshore renewables transaction of the year (detailed in the Industry section): TotalEnergies acquires Shell's 4 GW European pipeline while simultaneously recycling €1.8 billion in capital via KKR, reshaping the competitive map of utility-scale European renewables.

EU Battery Storage Is Ready for Its Moment in the Sun — 29 GW Fleet Primed for August 12 Eclipse; Totality at 8:28–8:32 PM Reduces Solar Dropout Risk

Ember publishes a pre-eclipse analysis of how Europe's dramatically expanded battery storage fleet changes the grid-management picture for the August 12 solar eclipse, when the path of totality crosses Spain, France, and Italy during evening peak hours. The total solar irradiance drop during the four-minute totality window will temporarily remove tens of gigawatts of solar generation from the grid, a ramp rate historically managed by fast-start gas turbines and pumped hydro. Science & ResearchНаука и исследования

Three papers push the frontier of machine learning weather forecasting this week. ECMWF's AIFS-SUBS (arXiv 2607.05100, Schuster et al.) is the first ML model deployed operationally at a major forecast center for sub-seasonal (weeks 2–6) prediction: it matches IFS probabilistic skill on MJO and NAO, extends useful MJO forecast lead by 8 days, and consumes 200× less energy at inference — addressing the most computationally expensive tier of global forecasting. At the other end of the timescale, Lehmann et al. (arXiv 2605.30184, ETH Zurich) systematically evaluate 9 AI weather models in year-long rollouts, documenting three distinct failure modes — numerical blow-up, slow climatological drift, and loss of seasonal cycle — and showing stability depends critically on the treatment of small spatio-temporal scales not well resolved by current architectures. Completing the theoretical framing, Dueben et al. (arXiv 2606.25076, ECMWF, CICERO, DKRZ) argue that ML is not merely replacing NWP modules but restructuring the entire forecasting value chain — model development, data exploitation, infrastructure, verification, and service delivery — and call for forecast centers to adopt new organizational models to capture the full benefit.
Три работы по ИИ-прогнозированию погоды. ECMWF представил AIFS-SUBS (arXiv 2607.05100): первая ML-модель оперативного центра для субсезонного прогноза (недели 2–6) — навык сопоставим с IFS, прогноз МДО продлён на 8 дней, расход энергии при инференсе в 200 раз ниже. Can AI Weather Models Predict Beyond Two Weeks? Year-Long Rollout Benchmark Reveals Three Failure Modes Across 9 Models (arXiv 2605.30184)

Lehmann, Ozdemir, Cheng, Hoefler, Schemm, Soja, and Mishra (ETH Zurich and collaborators) present a systematic evaluation of nine state-of-the-art AI weather models — including GraphCast, Pangu-Weather, FourCastNet, NeuralGCM, and AIFS — in year-long autoregressive rollouts, far beyond the typical 10–15 day evaluation window. The study documents three distinct failure modes that emerge over extended rollouts: numerical blow-up (abrupt model divergence, typically within weeks), slow climatological drift (gradual departure from climatological mean state, taking months to become apparent), and loss of seasonal cycle (failure to maintain the annual temperature and precipitation signal at long lead times). The analysis finds that model stability in long-range rollouts depends critically on how each model treats small spatio-temporal scales — models that smooth or truncate these scales tend to drift slowly, while those that preserve them risk blow-up. None of the nine models tested maintained full skill at the sub-seasonal range without some form of temporal recalibration.

Dueben, Bauer, Fuhrer, Koldunov, Kristiansen, and colleagues (ECMWF, CICERO, DKRZ) argue in this perspective paper that ML is not merely improving the accuracy of individual components within the numerical weather prediction pipeline but is fundamentally restructuring the entire forecasting value chain.
