AP / Las Vegas Sun 05 AUG
This Summer Isn't Breaking Heat Records Globally — Yet. Scientists Warn the Real Records Arrive When El Niño Peaks This Fall

An AP analysis explains why 2026 is currently tracking as the third-warmest year on record rather than the hottest: the El Niño signal propagates to global-mean atmospheric temperature with a roughly six-to-eight-week lag behind its ocean forcing, meaning the full impact of the event building in the tropical Pacific won't register in surface temperature anomalies until Q4. Scientists including former NASA chief climate scientist James Hansen project that once the lag resolves, the compounding effect of long-term warming and the intensifying El Niño will produce a surge in global mean temperature — potentially pushing 2026 past 2024's all-time annual record. Crucially, ocean temperatures are already showing the signal: global SSTs have been at or above 2024 record levels nearly every day since June, and 82% of the ocean surface is currently experiencing marine heatwave conditions per Copernicus Marine.

AP объясняет, почему 2026 г. пока на третьем месте, а не на первом: атмосферный отклик на Эль-Ниньо запаздывает на 6–8 недель. Когда задержка разрешится в Q4, эффект накопится: экс-главный климатолог NASA Джеймс Хансен прогнозирует, что 2026 год превзойдёт рекорд 2024 г. Уже сейчас SST на уровне рекордов 2024 года или выше — почти каждый день с июня; 82% поверхности океана — в режиме морских волн тепла по данным Copernicus Marine.

Mercator Ocean 01 AUG
Marine Heatwave Bulletin (1 August 2026): Active Systems Stable-to-Intensifying in Southern Ocean, Mid-Atlantic, and Bering Sea

Mercator Ocean International's weekly marine heatwave bulletin for August 1 documents ongoing active systems across four major ocean basins. In the Southern Ocean (50–80°E longitude, 45–60°S), a moderate-to-strong system remains stable in both area and intensity. In the mid-Atlantic, a large heatwave stretching from the central Atlantic to the Caribbean is decreasing in extent but intensifying north of the Caribbean — a divergent spatial pattern indicating internal reorganization rather than dissipation. Near the British Isles and west of Morocco, a separate Atlantic system is declining in extent but maintaining intensity. In the southern Bering Sea (~50°N), the North Pacific heatwave is expanding in area while sustaining moderate-to-strong intensity. This bulletin contextualizes the CMEMS H1 2026 mid-year assessment, which found that approximately 82% of the global ocean surface experienced marine heatwave conditions by end of June — the second-largest extent on record — with global mean SST at 20.94°C for the first half.

Еженедельный бюллетень Mercator Ocean (1 августа): активные морские тепловые волны в четырёх океанских бассейнах. В Южном океане (50–80°E, 45–60°S) — стабильная умеренно-сильная аномалия. В Атлантике система от центрального Атлантического океана до Карибского уменьшается в площади, но усиливается к северу от Карибов. Близ Британских островов — отдельная система снижается в площади, но сохраняет интенсивность. В южной части Берингова моря (~50°N) — расширение площади при стабильно сильной интенсивности.

Probabilistic Deep Learning for Drought Forecasting: Treating Internal Climate Variability as Signal, Not Noise (arXiv 2608.01864)

arXiv 2608.01864 · Funk, Gruber, Kauermann, Küchenhoff, Mittermeier · Preprint · 03 Aug 2026

Funk, Gruber, Kauermann, Küchenhoff, and Mittermeier present a probabilistic deep learning framework for drought forecasting that explicitly treats internal climate variability as structured, predictable signal rather than noise to be filtered out. The approach leverages the spatial, seasonal, and temporal structure of internal variability modes — including ENSO teleconnections and soil moisture memory — to improve skill at monthly to seasonal lead times. The paper directly addresses a recognized weakness in both operational seasonal outlooks and ML-based climate prediction: the tendency to average over variability modes that carry real forecast skill, leading to overconfident climatological means and underestimation of tail drought risk. The framework shows improved reliability and resolution relative to both climatological baselines and deterministic deep learning approaches across multiple biomes and lead times — a finding directly relevant to early warning systems and water resource planning under the current El Niño.

Satellite Data Reveal 20% Rise in Global Riverine Particulate Organic Carbon Export to Ocean Over 40 Years — Tropical Deforestation and Permafrost Thaw Drive Increase

Nature Geoscience · July 2026 · doi:10.1038/s41561-026-02034-w

A satellite-based study published in Nature Geoscience finds that global riverine particulate organic carbon (POC) export from land to ocean increased by approximately 20% over the past four decades — from approximately 144 ± 29 million tonnes per year in the 1980s to 171 ± 35 Mt yr⁻¹ in the 2010s. The analysis covers near-surface POC flux trends for 2,409 individual rivers globally, with 47% showing statistically significant temporal trends and 33% showing increasing trends. The primary drivers are tropical deforestation (accelerating erosion and mobilization of terrestrial organic matter) and Arctic permafrost thaw (releasing ancient frozen carbon into river systems that export it to the ocean). The 20% increase represents a substantial shift in the land-to-ocean organic carbon flux not yet fully captured in current Earth system models, with implications for the ocean's biological carbon pump efficiency and net CO₂ uptake — and for understanding the role of rivers as climate feedback amplifiers.

Can AI Weather Models Predict Beyond Two Weeks? Year-Long Rollout Benchmark Reveals Three Failure Modes Across 9 Models (arXiv 2605.30184)

arXiv 2605.30184 · Lehmann et al. · ETH Zurich · 05 Jun 2026

Lehmann et al. (ETH Zurich) systematically evaluate nine state-of-the-art AI weather models — including GraphCast, Pangu-Weather, FourCastNet, NeuralGCM, and AIFS — in year-long autoregressive rollouts, far beyond the typical 10–15 day evaluation window. Three distinct failure modes emerge: numerical blow-up (abrupt model divergence within weeks), slow climatological drift (gradual departure from mean state over months), and loss of seasonal cycle (failure to maintain the annual signal at long lead times). Model stability in long-range rollouts depends critically on treatment of small spatio-temporal scales — models that smooth these scales tend to drift slowly, while those that preserve them risk blow-up. None of the nine models maintained full skill at the sub-seasonal range without some form of temporal recalibration.
