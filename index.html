AMS / NOAA 10 AUG
AMS/NOAA "State of the Climate in 2025" Released — CO₂ Record 425.6 ppm, Ocean Heat Content and Sea Level at All-Time Highs, Arctic Second-Lowest Sea Ice, 38th Year of Glacier Loss

The AMS and NOAA's annual State of the Climate report (BAMS, August 10) synthesizes data from 60 countries to confirm 2025 as the warmest year in the 175-year instrumental record. Key findings: atmospheric CO₂ reached a record annual mean of 425.6 ppm, approximately 4 ppm above 2024; global ocean heat content set all-time highs across all measured depth layers (0–700m, 700–2000m, 2000–6000m); global mean sea level rose to a new record; Arctic sea ice area was the second-lowest for the annual minimum; and alpine glaciers recorded their 38th consecutive year of mass loss. Every major global temperature dataset independently confirmed 2025 as the warmest year, providing the peer-reviewed baseline for 2026 climate attribution and adaptation planning.

Ежегодный отчёт АМО/NOAA (10 августа) по данным 60 стран: 2025 — самый тёплый год за 175 лет. CO₂ — 425,6 ppm (рекорд); тепловой контент океана — новый максимум; уровень моря — рекорд; площадь арктического льда — вторая наименьшая; 38-й подряд год потери массы горных ледников.

The August 12 solar eclipse is crossing Europe today, with ENTSO-E's coordinated battery fleet — 29 GW of storage that doubled in a single year — managing the 9.7 GW photovoltaic dropout over roughly 75 minutes as the shadow traverses Spain, France, Germany, and Italy during evening peak hours. A NASA and IBM Research collaboration introduces Prithvi-Precip, a global precipitation forecasting system built on the Prithvi-WxC atmospheric foundation model. The study compares training targets derived from satellite-based precipitation estimates against reanalysis fields, and evaluates the impact of directly assimilating satellite observations into the model.
