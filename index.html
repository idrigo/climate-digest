Nature Climate Change 14 AUG
Failure to Track a Stable AMOC State Under Rapid Climate Change — Rate of Warming, Not Just Temperature Level, Determines Collapse Risk

Nature Climate Change · August 14, 2026 · DOI:10.1038/s41558-026-02730-w

A study published August 14 in Nature Climate Change demonstrates that the widely cited +4°C threshold for AMOC collapse is not a fixed target: Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation stability depends critically on the rate of radiative forcing change, not merely on the temperature level reached. Under a slow CO₂ ramp simulation, a stabilizing mechanism — enhanced ocean evaporation and reduced sea-ice extent — keeps the AMOC intact to +5.5°C of global warming. Under fast CO₂ ramps representing present-day or higher emission trajectories, the AMOC collapses at as little as +2°C. The finding implies that Paris Agreement compliance pathways cannot guarantee AMOC stability if warming occurs rapidly, fundamentally reframing what constitutes a "safe" trajectory for ocean circulation.

Nature Climate Change (14 авг): порог коллапса AMOC — не фиксированная температура. При медленном CO₂: стабильность до +5,5°C (испарение + сокращение льда компенсируют дестабилизацию). При быстром CO₂ (современные траектории): коллапс при +2°C. Вывод: соответствие Парижскому соглашению не гарантирует устойчивость AMOC — критически важный результат для климатических стратегий.

ECMWF 19 AUG
ECMWF Completing ORAS6 — Sixth-Generation Ensemble Ocean Reanalysis Extending to 1950 Forms Backbone of Forthcoming ERA6 Global Dataset

ECMWF · August 2026

ECMWF's sixth-generation ensemble ocean and sea-ice reanalysis system (ORAS6) is completing production of its historical data stream from 1950 to present in August 2026. ORAS6 advances over its predecessor ORAS5 by introducing ensemble-based data assimilation for the first time, allowing direct assimilation of sea-surface temperature observations and producing 11 ensemble members to quantify ocean state uncertainty. The system is forced by hourly ERA5 atmospheric fields and will serve as the ocean and sea-ice initial condition system for ERA6 — the forthcoming next-generation global atmospheric reanalysis produced by ECMWF for the EU Copernicus Climate Change Service. ERA6 is expected to eventually supersede ERA5 as the reference reanalysis for climate monitoring, impact assessment, and AI weather model training.

ECMWF (август 2026): завершается производство ORAS6 — ансамблевого реанализа океана и льда 6-го поколения (1950 — н.в.). Ключевые улучшения: ансамблевая ассимиляция, прямое включение ТПО, 11-элементный ансамбль. Основа для ERA6 (следующий глобальный атмосферный реанализ C3S) — будущая замена ERA5 для климатического мониторинга, оценки воздействий и обучения ИИ-моделей.
